```text
<title>
Apple Premium Motion Design Guide for Opus 5.5
</title>

<purpose>
Turn my brief and supplied assets into a finished premium motion video.
Let the brief determine its subject, audience, message, length, and format.
Communicate one clear idea and feeling, rather than showing everything at once.
</purpose>

<visual_system>
Define the visual rules before animating.
Use the supplied brand identity; otherwise choose up to three purposeful colors,
no more than two typefaces, consistent backgrounds, and a clear focal point.
Keep the surroundings quiet. Let the subject or UI carry the strongest color.
Use contrast to direct attention, not glow or decorative effects.
</visual_system>

<frame_design>
Storyboard the key shots as polished still frames first.
One shot = one idea. Give the main object space to breathe.
Make every frame readable at a glance; remove anything competing for attention.
Use warmer lifestyle imagery only when it adds meaning to the story.
Keep typography, color, and composition consistent across the video.
</frame_design>

<motion>
Animate with smooth easing and overlapping keyframes, not mechanical linear moves.
Let each transition grow naturally from the action or composition before it.
Use hard cuts only when they make the message stronger. Vary the rhythm: move quickly
when building energy, then hold important moments long enough for the viewer
to understand and feel them.
</motion>

<sound>
Choose music for the intended mood: 60-80 BPM for cinematic restraint, 90-110 for
effortless flow, or 115-123 for sophisticated energy. Synchronize meaningful visual
changes with the music. Add only subtle sound effects that clarify an action or
strengthen a key moment. Remove sounds that feel loud, generic, or disconnected
from the picture.
</sound>

<delivery>
Every font, color, movement, transition, and sound must have a reason. Build and
render the complete video, then watch it from beginning to end. Fix weak frames,
awkward timing, inconsistent styling, and distracting effects. Deliver the finished
video in the format requested by the brief. Do not stop at a mood board or storyboard.
If rendering is unavailable, provide precise shot-by-shot instructions needed to
produce the video.
</delivery>
```
